BORROWER'S RIGHT TO FREEZE OR REDUCE:
   Borrower may request to freeze or reduce the credit line at any time
   by contacting the HELOC Servicing Center. The credit line may be
   reopened or increased at Borrower's request, subject to Creditor
   approval.

CREDITOR'S RIGHT TO FREEZE, REDUCE, OR TERMINATE:
   Under federal law (Regulation Z, 12 CFR § 1026.40(f)), Creditor may
   freeze, reduce, or terminate the credit line if:

   (a) The property value declines significantly below the appraised value;
   (b) Creditor reasonably believes Borrower will not be able to repay;
   (c) There is a material change in Borrower's financial circumstances;
   (d) Borrower is in default under any term of this Agreement;
   (e) Government action (e.g., regulatory restriction) affects the plan;
   (f) The maximum APR is reached under the Agreement.

   If the credit line is frozen or reduced, Creditor must provide written
   notice and the reason. Borrower has the right to request reinstatement.

                    DEFAULT AND REMEDIES
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

EVENTS OF DEFAULT:
(a) Failure to make minimum payment within 30 days of due date;
(b) Failure to maintain insurance on the property;
(c) Failure to pay property taxes;
(d) Sale or transfer of the property;
(e) Bankruptcy filing;
(f) Material misrepresentation in the application;
(g) Destruction of the property;
(h) Default under the first mortgage.

REMEDIES:
Upon default, Creditor may:
(1) Freeze the credit line (no further advances);
(2) Accelerate the entire outstanding balance;
(3) Foreclose on the property (subject to the senior lien) through
    judicial foreclosure per Ohio Revised Code § 2323.07;
(4) Pursue a deficiency judgment for any balance remaining after sale.

NOTE: Ohio is a judicial foreclosure state. Creditor must obtain a court
order before conducting a foreclosure sale. Borrower has the right of
redemption under Ohio law.

IMPORTANT: Under the Tax Cuts and Jobs Act of 2017, interest paid on a
HELOC may only be deductible if the loan proceeds are used to "buy, build,
or substantially improve" the taxpayer's home that secures the loan.

Interest on HELOC funds used for debt consolidation, college tuition, or
other non-home-improvement purposes is generally NOT tax-deductible for
tax years 2018-2025. Consult a tax advisor.
