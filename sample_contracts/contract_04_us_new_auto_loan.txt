                    DEFAULT AND REPOSSESSION
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

DEFAULT: Buyer will be in default if:
(a) Buyer fails to make any payment when due;
(b) Buyer fails to maintain required insurance;
(c) Buyer provides false information on the credit application;
(d) Buyer attempts to sell or transfer the vehicle without consent;
(e) The vehicle is seized by any governmental authority.

REMEDIES UPON DEFAULT: Upon default, Creditor may:
(1) Demand immediate payment of the entire unpaid balance (acceleration);
(2) Repossess the vehicle as permitted by Texas Business & Commerce Code
    §9.609 (with or without judicial process, without breach of peace);
(3) Sell the vehicle at public or private sale after 10 days' notice;
(4) Seek a deficiency judgment for any remaining balance after sale.

RIGHT TO CURE: After default but before repossession, Buyer has the right
to cure the default by paying all past-due amounts plus late charges within
20 days of receiving written notice of default.

RIGHT OF REDEMPTION: After repossession, Buyer may redeem the vehicle by
paying the full accelerated balance plus repossession costs within 10 days.

INSURANCE REQUIREMENT: Buyer must maintain comprehensive and collision
insurance with Creditor listed as lienholder. Minimum coverage: actual cash
value of vehicle. If Buyer fails to maintain insurance, Creditor may purchase
force-placed insurance at Buyer's expense.
